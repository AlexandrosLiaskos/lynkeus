# Research note: Map, View and Share tabs, Contents pane and status bar

Before a function of this part is built, this note records for it:

1. How ArcGIS Pro and QGIS offer the same function: its name, its place and its behaviour, with the address of
   the documentation page.
2. The existing function of OpenLayers or of the U.S. Web Design System that provides it, with its version and
   the address of its documentation.
3. The limits found, and whatever could not be confirmed.
4. The date on which each source was read.

The sections below belong to the Map tab. OpenLayers is version 10.11.0 throughout, and its behaviour was read
in the code of the npm package `ol` (the files named in each section) on 14 Nov 2026.

## Keys of the map, previous and next extent, rotation

| | |
|---|---|
| ArcGIS Pro | The arrow keys move the view. The comma key goes to the previous extent and the period key to the next one, the A key rotates the view counterclockwise and the D key clockwise, the N key adjusts the view to point north, and the Insert key zooms to the full extent ([ArcGIS Pro keyboard shortcuts](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/3.3/get-started/arcgis-pro-keyboard-shortcuts.htm)). Every change of the extent of the map is stored, so that the user can go backward and forward through a list of extents ([Navigation](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/help/mapping/navigation/navigation-in-arcgis-pro.htm)). |
| QGIS | Zoom Last zooms the map to the previous extent in history and Zoom Next to the next one ([2D Map View](https://docs.qgis.org/4.2/en/docs/user_manual/map_views/map_view.html)). The rotation of the map in QGIS was not read. |
| Function used | The default interactions of OpenLayers (`ol/interaction/defaults.js`): `KeyboardPan` moves the map by 128 pixels with an arrow key, `KeyboardZoom` changes the zoom by one level with the plus and the minus key, `DragRotate` turns the map while the Alt and Shift keys are held, and `PinchRotate` turns it with two fingers. The map listens to the keys only while its element has the keyboard focus, and it ignores a key that is pressed inside its own controls (`ol/Map.js`). The event `moveend` of the map marks each view at which the map comes to rest. `View.animate` moves and turns the view; a positive rotation is clockwise (`ol/View.js`). |
| Choices of Lynkeus | The keys comma, period, A, D, N and Insert are added with one interaction (`web/src/map/keys.ts`) and act only while the map has the keyboard focus, as criterion 2.1.4 of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines asks of keys that are single characters. One press of the Rotate button, or of the A or D key, turns the map by 15 degrees. The list of views keeps the latest 100. A movement takes 250 milliseconds. These three numbers come from no source. |
| Limits | A view is recorded when the map comes to rest, so the views passed during one movement are not recorded. The Rotate button turns clockwise only; the other direction needs the A key or the pointer. |
| Tried in a browser | Chromium, through Playwright 1.65.1, on a private copy of the page under the security policy of the site, 14 Nov 2026, in a window of 1440 by 900 pixels. After Previous extent and Next extent the view was the one that had been left, to 0.00001 degree. After two presses of Rotate the smallest upright rectangle around the view was 11.42289 degrees wide, against 11.42291 degrees computed for a view of 1440 by 745 pixels that is turned by 30 degrees. The N key, and the D key followed by the A key, returned the view exactly. The Tab key alone reached the Rectangle button and the map. No request failed, and the console held no error. Not tried: the Insert key, and turning with the pointer or with two fingers. |

## Area

| | |
|---|---|
| ArcGIS Pro and QGIS | Not read for this function: neither program has one area that all its tools share. The extent parameter of a geoprocessing tool in ArcGIS Pro was not read. |
| Function used | `Draw` of OpenLayers with the geometry function `createBox()` draws a rectangle from two clicks (`ol/interaction/Draw.js`). `Translate` moves a feature that the pointer drags and reports the start, each step and the end of the movement (`ol/interaction/Translate.js`). The interaction that was added last receives an event first (`ol/Map.js`), so a press on the rectangle moves it and does not start a new one. `Modify` was read and not used: it moves one vertex of a polygon, which turns a rectangle into another shape. The interaction `Extent` was read and not used: it resizes a rectangle by its sides and corners but does not move it. `DragBox` was read and not used: it draws with a drag only, and it leaves nothing on the map. |
| Choices of Lynkeus | The layer `area` draws the area whenever `areaStore` holds one, whichever part of the interface has set it: a dark dashed line on a white line, without a fill, so that the layers inside it keep their colours. While the Rectangle button is on, the rectangle carries eight handles (four corners and the middle of four sides), each of which is a feature that `Translate` moves; the rectangle is computed again from the handle that moves. A handle is a square of 10 pixels that the pointer grabs within 8 pixels around it, which gives a target of 26 pixels (criterion 2.5.8 asks for 24). The sides are rounded to five decimals of a degree, about one metre. From the current view takes the smallest rectangle along the meridians and the parallels that holds the view, also on a map that is turned. These numbers come from no source. |
| Without a pointer | While a tool is in use and the keyboard has brought the focus to the map, a cross marks the centre of the map. The arrow keys move the map under the cross, and the Enter key sets a corner at the cross; the second corner ends the rectangle. The Escape key gives up a rectangle that is being drawn or, when none is, ends the tool. Moving and resizing by dragging have the form of From coordinates as their alternative without dragging (criterion 2.5.7). A sentence with the role `status`, kept off the screen, tells a screen reader the sides of the area and that the first corner is set. |
| Limits | An area does not cross the meridian of 180 degrees (`isArea` in `web/src/shared/area.ts`). The map repeats the world towards the east and the west; a rectangle that is drawn in a repeated world is stored with its longitudes between -180 and 180 degrees, a rectangle that crosses that meridian is refused with a sentence, and a rectangle wider than the world becomes the whole range of longitude. Web Mercator ends at 85.0511 degrees of latitude, so an area drawn on the map does not reach the poles; From coordinates accepts latitudes up to 90 degrees. |
| Tried in a browser | Chromium, through Playwright 1.65.1, on a private copy of the page under the security policy of the site, 14 Nov 2026, in a window of 1440 by 900 pixels, and of 390 by 800 pixels. With the view at 1,739.367 metres for each pixel, From the current view gave west -11.25, south 14.44323, east 11.25, north 25.36745, against -11.25, 14.443229, 11.25 and 25.367447 computed from the size of the map with the formulas of Web Mercator. A rectangle drawn with two clicks, resized by its north-east handle, resized by its west handle and moved by dragging gave each time the sides computed from the pixels of the pointer; the largest difference was 0.0000043 degree, which is the rounding to five decimals. With the keyboard alone (the Enter key, two steps east, one step south, the Enter key) the area was 0, 18.10969, 4, 20, against 0, 18.109693, 4 and 20 computed. The Escape key gave up a rectangle and then ended the tool. A view across the meridian of 180 degrees was refused with its sentence, and the area stayed. Zoom to area filled the view up to its margin of 40 pixels. From coordinates set -71.2, 42.6, -70.75, 42.95 exactly and refused a west side of -60. At 390 pixels a rectangle of two clicks agreed to 0.000005 degree, and the page did not scroll sideways. The map was drawn 745 pixels high in an element of 744.5625 pixels, so the browser shrank it by 0.06 percent in height; the computed sides allow for it. |

## Graticule

| | |
|---|---|
| ArcGIS Pro and QGIS | In ArcGIS Pro a graticule belongs to the page layout. QGIS also has a grid as a decoration of the map view. The section [Lines and values of the graticule](#lines-and-values-of-the-graticule) records what was read of both. |
| Function used | The layer `Graticule` of OpenLayers (`ol/layer/Graticule.js`). It chooses the interval between the lines from a list (90, 45, 30, 20, 10, 5, 2 and 1 degrees, then 30, 20, 10, 5, 2 and 1 minutes, then the same seconds) so that the cells are about 100 pixels wide, draws at most 100 meridians and parallels, repeats with the world, and writes the value of each line in degrees, minutes and seconds with the letter of the hemisphere. |
| Choices of Lynkeus | Two layers of the same lines in one group with the id `graticule`: a white line 2 pixels wide under a dark line 1 pixel wide, so that the lines stay visible on a background map of any colour (criterion 1.4.11). The labels are dark text with a white edge of 1 pixel, in the fonts of the page. The section [Lines and values of the graticule](#lines-and-values-of-the-graticule) gives the reasons for these widths. |
| Limits | The lines are those of longitude and latitude in WGS 84. The interval cannot be chosen by the user. |
| Tried in a browser | Chromium, through Playwright 1.65.1, on a private copy of the page under the security policy of the site, 14 Nov 2026, in a window of 1440 by 900 pixels: the lines and their values showed over the Blue Marble on a map with north at the top and on a map turned by 30 degrees. The screenshots were looked at; nothing was measured. |

| | |

The same layer of VIIRS on Suomi NPP ends on 2026-11-02 in that document and is left out. |
| Requests tried | From the server of Lynkeus, 14 Nov 2026. A tile of each of the four layers for 2026-11-02 (zoom 6, row 23, column 19): status 200, `image/jpeg`, 13,039 to 16,468 bytes. A tile of zoom level 9: status 200. A tile of zoom level 10: status 400. A day after the present day (2027-01-01) and a day before the first one (1999-01-01): status 404. Every tile carried `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, also a tile of the Blue Marble and, with an `Origin` header, a tile of `tile.openstreetmap.org`. |
| Function used | The source `XYZ` of OpenLayers with `maxZoom: 9`, and `setUrl` when the user chooses another layer or day (`ol/source/XYZ.js`, `ol/source/UrlTile.js`). The three background sources carry `crossOrigin: "anonymous"`, so that the browser lets the page read the drawn map; `ol/source/OSM.js` sets it by default. |
| Choices of Lynkeus | The imagery of a date is a third background map with the id `imagery`. Its form starts with MODIS on Terra and the day before the present day in Coordinated Universal Time. |
| Limits | The first day of each layer is written in `web/src/map/imagery.ts` as read on 14 Nov 2026; the page cannot ask the service for it, because the security policy of the site lets the page request data only from the site itself. The days that a layer lacks between its first and its last day are not known to the form. The images are true-colour pictures for orientation, not data that Lynkeus analyses. |
| Tried in a browser | Chromium, through Playwright 1.65.1, on a private copy of the page under the security policy of the site, 14 Nov 2026, in a window of 1440 by 900 pixels: the button opened its pane, the day 2026-11-02 of MODIS on Terra was shown, the tiles of `MODIS_Terra_CorrectedReflectance_TrueColor/default/2026-11-02/GoogleMapsCompatible_Level9/` arrived with status 200, the line of the source named the day, and Day before requested the tiles of 2026-11-01. ## Go to coordinates and bookmarks

| | |
|---|---|
| ArcGIS Pro | Go To XY, in the Navigate group of the Map tab, shows a movable overlay at the bottom of the view with boxes for the longitude and the latitude; the button removes the overlay again. Coordinates are typed or pasted in decimal degrees ("118.8348591°W 33.6337148°N"), in degrees, minutes and seconds ("118°50'45.0"W 33°38'1.05"N") and in other formats; the letters E, W, N and S name the hemisphere. The tool flashes the place, pans to it and can mark it with a point ([Go To XY tool](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/help/mapping/navigation/go-to-xy.htm), [Navigate maps and scenes](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/get-started/navigate-your-data.htm)). Bookmarks are created and used from the Map tab, and the Bookmarks pane manages them, for example to update or remove one ([Navigation](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/help/mapping/navigation/navigation-in-arcgis-pro.htm)). |
| QGIS | Spatial bookmarks save a place under a name; the Spatial Bookmarks panel uses and manages them, and they are stored in an XML file ([2D Map View](https://docs.qgis.org/4.2/en/docs/user_manual/map_views/map_view.html)). |
| Function used | `View.animate` moves the map; the mark is a point feature on the layer `mark`. The bookmarks are kept with `localStorage` of the browser under the name `lynkeus.bookmarks`. |
| Choices of Lynkeus | A latitude or a longitude is read as decimal degrees, as degrees and minutes, or as degrees, minutes and seconds, with the letter of the hemisphere or a minus sign (`web/src/map/coordinates.ts`). The map moves to the place and keeps its scale, as Pan does in ArcGIS Pro. A bookmark holds the centre in degrees, the zoom level and the rotation; its name has at most 60 characters and is not used twice. The number 60 comes from no source. |
| Limits | The other formats of ArcGIS Pro (MGRS, UTM, USNG) are not read. The bookmarks stay in one browser on one computer; they cannot be exported. A latitude beyond 85.0511 degrees is refused, because the map in Web Mercator ends there. |
| Tried in a browser | Chromium, through Playwright 1.65.1, on a private copy of the page under the security policy of the site, 14 Nov 2026, in a window of 1440 by 900 pixels. Go to coordinates with "42° 48′ 30″ N" and "70.9 W" centred the view on longitude -70.9 and on the latitude 42.80833 to 0.31 metre of the projection, marked the place and reported it; a latitude of 86 was refused. A bookmark returned the map to its view to 0.00001 degree, was still listed after the page was loaded again, was renamed with the Enter key and removed. At 390 pixels the pane covered the map, moved it and closed again. |

## Measure distance and area

| | |
|---|---|
| ArcGIS Pro | The measure tools are in the Inquiry group of the Map tab. A measure window appears on top of the map, in which the user sets the units and the mode: planar, which uses 2D Cartesian mathematics, or geodesic, which follows the surface of the spheroid. Units can be set for the whole and for each segment. The Esc key cancels the sketch, and Clear Results resets the results ([Measure](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/help/mapping/navigation/measure.htm), [Geodesic versus planar distance](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/tool-reference/spatial-analyst/geodesic-versus-planar-distance.htm)). |
| QGIS | Measure Line and Measure Area measure by default on the ellipsoid of the project, or in Cartesian mode; the measure window shows each segment and the total ([2D Map View](https://docs.qgis.org/4.2/en/docs/user_manual/map_views/map_view.html)). |
| Function used | `getDistance` and `getArea` of `ol/sphere.js`. They measure on a sphere with a radius of 6,371,008.8 metres, the mean radius of the WGS 84 ellipsoid: a distance is the great circle between two points (haversine formula), and an area is computed from the corners of the polygon with a formula that the documentation of OpenLayers describes as the area of the polygon whose sides are great circles (see the limits). `Draw` draws the line or the polygon; its methods `appendCoordinates`, `removeLastPoint`, `finishDrawing` and `abortDrawing` serve the keyboard. `greatCircleArc` of `ol/geom/flat/geodesic.js`, which the graticule of OpenLayers uses, gives the points of each segment, so the line on the map is the line that is measured. |
| Choices of Lynkeus | Lengths in metres (one decimal) or kilometres (three decimals); areas in square metres (no decimal), hectares (two decimals) or square kilometres (three decimals). The numbers of decimals come from no source. A planar mode is not offered: on a map in Web Mercator it would give lengths that are too long by the secant of the latitude. |
| Comparison | Against pyproj 3.8.0 with PROJ 9.8.1, `Geod(ellps="WGS84")`, 14 Nov 2026. Distances: (10 E, 1 N) to (11 E, 2 N) 157,225.649 m against 156,876.149 m (+0.22 %); the diagonal of the sample box, (71.20 W, 42.60 N) to (70.75 W, 42.95 N), 53,512.963 m against 53,553.139 m (-0.08 %); (20 E, 78 N) to (25 E, 79 N) 156,908.494 m against 157,570.158 m (-0.42 %); (74 W, 40.7 N) to (3.7 W, 40.4 N) 5,769,215.291 m against 5,783,802.573 m (-0.25 %). Areas of boxes with their corners joined by geodesics: the sample box 1,429.429 km² against 1,431.864 km² (-0.17 %); 10 to 11 E, 1 to 2 N 12,359.952 km² against 12,305.129 km² (+0.45 %); 20 to 25 E, 78 to 79 N 12,325.114 km² against 12,414.459 km² (-0.72 %). The largest difference found is 0.42 % for a length and 0.72 % for an area. |
| Limits | The sphere differs from the ellipsoid: with pyproj, a line of one degree is longer on the sphere by 0.56 % along a meridian at the equator and shorter by 0.45 % near a pole, and a box of one degree is larger by 0.45 % at the equator and smaller by 0.89 % near a pole. The pane states it as up to 0.6 percent for a length and 0.9 percent for an area. The area of OpenLayers is exact only for a small polygon: for a polygon of about 677 by 424 kilometres at 20 degrees north it gave 270,172.009 km², against 270,337.202 km² from pyproj for the polygon with great-circle sides on the same sphere (-0.06 %), while its perimeter agreed to 0.04 metre. A segment longer than half the circumference of the Earth is measured along the shorter way. Heights are not used. |
| Tried in a browser | Chromium, through Playwright 1.65.1, on a private copy of the page under the security policy of the site, 14 Nov 2026, in a window of 1440 by 900 pixels, beside the open pane. A line of three clicks gave segments of 570.268 km and 588.120 km and a whole of 1,158.389 km (1,158,388.6 m in metres); the great circles between the clicked places, computed with the haversine formula from the pixels, give 570.268 km, 588.120 km and 1,158.389 km. A polygon of four clicks gave a perimeter of 2,121,340.4 m, against 2,121,340.4 m computed, and 270,172.009 km² (27,017,200.93 ha). The check itself had expected other numbers, because it took the resolution of the view without the factor 1.03962 by which OpenLayers lets a view pass its limit after a movement (`getSmoothClampedResolution` in `ol/resolutionconstraint.js`); the numbers above were computed afterwards with that factor. Clear results emptied the list and the map. Not tried in a browser: the measure tools with the keyboard, which the tests cover. |

## Zoom to layer, Identify and Swipe

These three functions depend on the Contents pane, which holds the selected layer and draws the layers.

| | |
|---|---|
| ArcGIS Pro | The Swipe tool is in the Compare group of the tab of a layer. It needs the layer to be selected in the Contents pane, hides that layer interactively and reveals what lies beneath it; the user drags on the map from the top or from a side ([Imagery appearance](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/help/data/imagery/raster-display-ribbon.htm), [Change the appearance of a feature layer](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/2.6/help/mapping/layer-properties/change-the-appearance-of-a-feature-layer.htm), [Explore imagery](https://learn.arcgis.com/en/projects/explore-imagery-spatial-resolution/)). The pop-up of the Explore tool and the Locate pane were not read. |
| QGIS | The Identify Features tool returns information on the features or the pixels of a clicked place in the Identify Results window; Zoom To Layer zooms to the extent of the active layer ([General Tools](https://docs.qgis.org/4.2/en/docs/user_manual/introduction/general_tools.html)). A swipe tool of QGIS was not read. |
| Standard | The swipe line follows the slider pattern: an element with the role `slider` and the properties `aria-valuenow`, `aria-valuemin` and `aria-valuemax`, with `aria-orientation` set to vertical when it moves up and down. The right and the up arrow increase its value by one step and the left and the down arrow decrease it, Home and End set the first and the last value, and Page Up and Page Down change it by a larger amount ([ARIA Authoring Practices Guide, Slider Pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/slider/)). |
| Function used | `extentOf`, `valueAt` and `findLayer` of `web/src/contents/display.ts`, and `selectedLayerStore` and `displayOf` of `web/src/contents/state.ts`. For the swipe, the events `prerender` and `postrender` of a layer of OpenLayers, with `getRenderPixel` of `ol/render.js`: a layer that is drawn with WebGL is cut with `gl.scissor`, and a layer that is drawn on a canvas with a clip path. |
| Choices of Lynkeus | Identify lists the visible layers of the Contents pane, the top one first, with the quantity, the value in six significant digits, the unit and the dates, above them the latitude and the longitude of the place with five decimals, and marks the place. The value of the slider is the share of the map that the swiped layer covers: the layer is drawn on the left of an upright line and under a level line, so the arrows that increase the value move the line to the right or up. The line moves by 1 percent of the map with an arrow key and by 10 percent with Page Up and Page Down. These numbers come from no source. |
| Limits | The value is the one that the layer has drawn (see `valueAt`): at a small scale it is the mean of the cells that one drawn cell covers. A place is identified only on the layers of the Contents pane, not on the background map. |
| Tried | Tests only. These functions were written after the browser checks on a private copy had ended, so none of them was tried in a browser: the reading of a value from a layer drawn with WebGL, the cut of a layer along the swipe line, and the dragging of the line. The comparison of the values of five places of a sample file with the values read from the file with GDAL was not made. |

## Lines and values of the graticule

| | |
|---|---|
| ArcGIS Pro | A graticule is an element of a layout. Its gridlines, ticks and labels each have a symbol that the user can change ([Graticules](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/help/layouts/add-and-modify-graticules.htm)), and the tutorial adds the style named Black Horizontal Label Graticule ([Tutorial: Work with a graticule](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/help/layouts/work-with-a-grid.htm)). A text symbol can carry a halo, whose size is given in points ([Text symbol properties](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/help/mapping/layer-properties/text-symbol-properties.htm)). The default width of the gridlines, and whether the labels of a graticule carry a halo by default, were not found in these pages, which were read through a web search. |
| QGIS | The grid decoration of the map view draws a line symbol and, when Draw annotations is ticked, the coordinates in a text format, which can carry a buffer ([2D Map View, Decorating the map](https://docs.qgis.org/3.44/en/docs/user_manual/map_views/map_view.html)). The defaults were read in the source code of QGIS (branch `master`, commit `534b2f6` of 14 Nov 2026). A grid decoration without saved settings takes the default line symbol and the default text format (`src/app/decorations/qgsdecorationgrid.cpp`). The default line is solid, 0.26 millimetres wide, in the colour RGB 35, 35, 35 (`DEFAULT_SIMPLELINE_COLOR` and `DEFAULT_SIMPLELINE_WIDTH` in `src/core/symbology/qgslinesymbollayer.h`, `Qgis::DEFAULT_LINE_WIDTH` in `src/core/qgis.cpp`). At 96 pixels to the inch that is 0.98 pixel. The buffer of a default text format is switched off; when the user switches it on, it is white and 1 millimetre wide (`QgsTextBufferSettingsPrivate` in `src/core/textrenderer/qgstextrenderer_p.h`). |
| OpenLayers 10.11.0 | `ol/layer/Graticule.js`, read in the npm package. Without a style the lines are black at 20 % opacity and 1 pixel wide, and the labels are black text of 12 pixels with a white stroke of 3 pixels, of which 1.5 pixels show outside the letters. |
| Choices of Lynkeus | QGIS draws one dark line of about 1 pixel without an edge and labels without a buffer, and leaves the colours to the user, who knows the map at hand. Lynkeus has no settings for the graticule and has background maps of opposite brightness, so it keeps a white edge and makes it thin. A line is a dark line 1 pixel wide on a white line 2 pixels wide, which leaves a white edge of half a pixel on each side. Before, the white line was 3 pixels wide at 70 % opacity. The text of a value has a white stroke of 2 pixels, of which 1 pixel shows outside the letters; before, the stroke was 3 pixels wide, the default of OpenLayers. The colours are "ink" (#1b1b1b) and "white" (#ffffff) of the U.S. Web Design System. The two widths of 2 pixels come from no source: they are the smallest whole numbers of pixels that leave an edge. |
| Contrast of the values | Computed with the formula of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.2. The dark text has a contrast of 17.22 to 1 with its white edge, on any map. Against the map itself, either the text or its edge has the higher contrast. On a background of any grey the higher of the two is at least 4.15 to 1; it is weakest on a grey of relative luminance 0.20. On the Blue Marble the dark text alone has less than 3 to 1 on 72.9 % of the pixels (median 1.32 to 1), the white edge has a median of 13.09 to 1, and the higher of the two is 4.15 to 1 at the least, 8.07 to 1 at the fifth percentile and 15.26 to 1 at the median. On OpenStreetMap two colours cover 96.9 % of the pixels, the sea (#aad3df) and the land (#f2efe9). The dark text has 10.74 to 1 and 15.01 to 1 with them, and the white edge 1.60 to 1 and 1.15 to 1, so the edge does not show there and is not needed. On the image of MODIS on Terra of 13 Nov 2026 the higher of the two is 4.15 to 1 at the least, 5.12 to 1 at the fifth percentile and 15.67 to 1 at the median. |
| Contrast of the lines | The drawing of a vertical line was computed for a screen on which one pixel of the page is one pixel of the screen. Each stroke covers a share of a pixel and is laid over what is there, the white line first and the dark line on it. The figure is the strongest contrast of a pixel of the line with the background, for a line centred on a pixel, for one on the border of two pixels, and for one half way between. Without the white edge the line has 1.07 to 1.18 to 1 on the most frequent sea colour of the Blue Marble (#020514), so the edge cannot be left out. With the white line of 2 pixels it has 5.30 to 6.97 to 1 there. With the white of 70 % opacity that was used before, a white line of 2 pixels would have 3.08 to 3.93 to 1, which is why the white is now opaque. Over 200,000 pixels of the Blue Marble taken at random, a line centred on a pixel has less than 3 to 1 on 0.7 % of them (median 5.18 to 1); the lines of before had less than 3 to 1 on none (median 8.98 to 1). On the sea and the land of OpenStreetMap a dark line centred on a pixel has 10.74 to 1 and 15.01 to 1. A line on the border of two pixels is drawn there as two grey columns, with 2.07 to 1 and 2.89 to 1; the lines of before had 2.29 to 1 and 2.98 to 1 in the same place. |
| Limits | The contrast of the lines is a computation, not a measurement of what a browser draws. A browser may smooth a stroke in another way, and on a screen with two pixels for each pixel of the page the white edge is one whole pixel of the screen. On a part of an image that is neither dark nor light, a line on the border of two pixels is hard to see; this was so before the change as well. |

The tiles were requested from the server of Lynkeus: `BlueMarble_ShadedRelief_Bathymetry` and `MODIS_Terra_CorrectedReflectance_TrueColor` of 2026-11-13 from NASA GIBS, and the tiles of `tile.openstreetmap.org`. For each background the 16 tiles of zoom level 2 were taken, which is the zoom level of the first view: 1,048,576 pixels. The colours were read with Pillow 13.0.0 and the contrasts computed with NumPy 2.4.6. The tests compute the contrast of the two colours and the weakest case over the greys. |
